TITLE:
What If the Moon Crashed Into Earth? 🌕💥 #Shorts

DESCRIPTION:
What would actually happen if the Moon crashed into Earth? The answer is wilder than you think.

The Moon is about 239,000 miles (384,400 km) away. Now imagine pulling it closer.

🌊 At half the distance, the Moon looks twice as wide in the sky, and its pull on our oceans gets 8 times stronger. High tides flood entire coastlines. New York, Miami and Shanghai end up underwater twice a day.

🌋 Keep pulling, and the Moon's gravity starts tugging on the ground itself. Earthquakes everywhere. Volcanoes erupting all at once.

💥 About 11,000 miles out, it reaches the Roche limit. Earth pulls much harder on the Moon's near side than its far side, and that difference tears the Moon apart. So it never hits us in one piece.

🪐 The fragments spread into a giant ring around Earth, just like Saturn's. Beautiful... for a little while.

🔥 Then the pieces start falling. Massive chunks rain down every day, the sky glows red-hot, the oceans boil away and the crust melts into a sea of magma. Earth becomes a planet of fire.

🌑 The twist: something like this already happened. About 4.5 billion years ago, a Mars-sized planet called Theia slammed into the young Earth. The debris from that collision formed the Moon.

So the Moon was born from a collision. Let's just hope it never comes home.

📚 Science notes
• Tidal force scales with the inverse cube of distance, so half the distance means 8× the pull.
• The Roche limit for the Moon is roughly 18,000 km (about 11,000 miles) from Earth's center.
• The giant-impact hypothesis is the leading scientific explanation for how the Moon formed.
• The Moon is actually drifting away from Earth by about 3.8 cm (1.5 inches) every year, so don't worry.

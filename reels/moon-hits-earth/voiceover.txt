Not a meteor. Not an asteroid. The Moon. Seventy-three quintillion tons of rock, heading straight for us.

At half its current distance, the Moon looks twice as wide in the sky. And its pull on our oceans? Eight times stronger. High tide doesn't just come in anymore. It floods entire coastlines. New York, Miami, Shanghai. Underwater, twice a day.

Keep pulling. The Moon's gravity starts tugging on the ground itself. Earthquakes everywhere. Volcanoes waking up all at once.

Then, about eleven thousand miles out, something wild happens. Earth pulls so much harder on the near side of the Moon than the far side, that it rips the Moon apart.

So it never actually hits us in one piece. Instead, it shatters into trillions of fragments, and forms a giant ring around Earth, just like Saturn's. Beautiful? Absolutely. For a little while.

Because those pieces start falling. Massive chunks rain down, every single day. The sky glows red hot. The oceans boil away. The crust melts into a sea of magma.

Earth becomes a planet of fire. Nothing survives.

And here's the crazy part. Scientists think this has happened before. Four and a half billion years ago, a Mars-sized planet slammed into the young Earth. And the debris from that crash? That's what formed the Moon.

So the Moon was born from a collision. Let's just hope it never comes home.

Imagine the sun rising on you sixteen times a day.

Just a normal day... if you're an astronaut on the International Space Station.

The station circles the Earth every ninety minutes, at twenty-eight thousand kilometers per hour, four hundred kilometers above our heads.

Six a.m. The alarm goes off. And you've been sleeping... upright. Yep. Standing up. Strapped to the wall, so you don't float away in your sleep.

First up: your teeth. Toothpaste? You swallow it... or spit in a towel. No sink.

Your drinking water? About ninety-eight percent of it is recycled... from sweat and urine. And relax. It's cleaner than most tap water.

Breakfast? No bread. Crumbs float into the equipment. So it's tortillas. Salt and pepper? Liquid. So they don't float away.

Then it's work: hundreds of science experiments. And you're part of the experiment.

Your body changes up there. Without gravity, your bones lose about one percent a month. So you work out two hours every day.

And you get taller. Your spine stretches out, up to five centimeters. But... it disappears back on Earth.

At day's end, you head to the Cupola, the biggest window on the station. Below you: continents, clouds... and night sweeping across the planet.

And it hits you: everything you've ever known... everyone you love... lives on this one small blue dot.

It's nine-thirty at night. Back into the sleeping bag... and the sun rises again, before you've even fallen asleep.

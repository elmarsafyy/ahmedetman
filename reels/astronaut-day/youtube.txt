TITLE:
A Day in the Life of an Astronaut on the ISS 🚀 16 Sunrises a Day #Shorts

DESCRIPTION:
Imagine the sun rising on you 16 times a day. For astronauts on the International Space Station, that's just a normal day.

🛰️ The ISS circles Earth every 90 minutes at about 28,000 km/h (17,500 mph), roughly 400 km (250 miles) above our heads.

⏰ 6:00 a.m. GMT. The alarm goes off. Astronauts sleep "upright" in sleeping bags strapped to the wall, so they don't drift around the station in their sleep. In microgravity, there's no up or down.

🪥 Brushing your teeth? There's no sink. You either swallow the toothpaste or spit it into a towel.

💧 About 98% of the water on board is recycled, including from sweat and urine. And it's cleaner than a lot of tap water on Earth.

🌮 No bread. Crumbs float away and can get into the equipment, so astronauts eat tortillas instead. Salt and pepper come in liquid form so they don't float off.

🔬 Then it's work: hundreds of scientific experiments, from growing plants to studying the human body. And the astronauts are part of the experiment themselves.

🦴 Without gravity, bones can lose about 1% of their mass every month. That's why astronauts exercise around 2 hours a day.

📏 Your spine stretches out, and you can grow up to about 5 cm (2 inches) taller. But it's gone soon after you're back on Earth.

🌍 At the end of the day, there's the Cupola, the station's biggest window: continents, clouds, and night sweeping across the planet. That's where it hits you. Everything you've ever known and everyone you love lives on one small blue dot.

🌙 9:30 p.m. Back into the sleeping bag... and the sun rises again before you've even fallen asleep.

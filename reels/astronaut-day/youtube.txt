TITLE:
A Day in the Life of a NASA Astronaut on the ISS 🚀 #Shorts

DESCRIPTION:
What's a normal day like for an astronaut on the International Space Station? Picture watching the sun rise 16 times before dinner. 🌅🚀

🛰️ The ISS whips around Earth every 90 minutes at about 17,500 mph, roughly 250 miles above our heads. That's fast enough to cross the entire U.S. in about 10 minutes.

⏰ 6:00 a.m. (GMT, the station's time zone). The alarm goes off. Astronauts sleep "standing up" in sleeping bags strapped to the wall, so they don't drift around while they sleep. In zero gravity, there's no up or down.

🪥 Brushing your teeth? There's no sink up there. You either swallow the toothpaste or spit it into a towel.

💧 About 98% of the water on board gets recycled, including from sweat and urine. Before you say "gross": it's cleaner than a lot of tap water back home.

🌮 No bread on the ISS. Crumbs float away and can get into the equipment, so astronauts use tortillas instead. (Yes, they make space tacos.) Salt and pepper come in liquid form so they don't float off.

🔬 Then it's time to work: hundreds of science experiments, from growing lettuce in space to studying the human body. And the astronauts themselves are part of the experiment.

🦴 Without gravity, your bones can lose about 1% of their mass every month. That's why astronauts work out around 2 hours a day on special treadmills and resistance machines.

📏 Your spine stretches out, and you can come back up to 2 inches taller! But it doesn't last. You shrink back once you're home on Earth.

🌍 At the end of the day, there's the Cupola, the biggest window on the station. Continents, clouds, hurricanes, city lights, and night sweeping across the planet. That's when it hits you: everything you've ever known and everyone you love lives on one small blue dot.

🌙 9:30 p.m. Back into the sleeping bag... and the sun rises again before you've even fallen asleep.

⚡ Quick facts
• The ISS orbits Earth about 16 times a day.
• Speed: about 17,500 mph. Altitude: about 250 miles.
• It has been continuously crewed since November 2000.
• About 98% of the station's water is recycled.
• Astronauts exercise about 2 hours a day to fight bone and muscle loss.

Flip the map. Every ocean becomes land and every continent becomes ocean. Sounds cool, right? It would be a nightmare. Right now, water covers 71% of Earth. Flip it and we're living on a giant dry rock with just a few lonely seas. And the oceans are deep. The average seafloor sits 3.7 kilometers down. Average land, less than one kilometer up. Drop Mount Everest into the Mariana Trench and its peak is still two kilometers underwater. Here's the scary part. About 86% of the water that becomes rain evaporates from the ocean. Shrink the oceans and the rain disappears. The center of these megacontinents would be thousands of kilometers from any coast. Picture deserts that make the Sahara look like a lawn, with summers like an oven and winters worse than Siberia. Oceans also absorb over 90% of the extra heat we trap. And tiny ocean plankton make about half the oxygen you're breathing right now. Fewer oceans means fewer plankton and less air. Earth already tested this. The supercontinent Pangea had a scorched, bone-dry heart. So the blue on the map isn't empty space. It's Earth's life support. Follow Brain Loop. Keep your brain looping.
